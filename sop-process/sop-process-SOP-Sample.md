# Standard Operating Procedure
## Well Site Equipment Lockout/Tagout Prior to Maintenance

**Document Owner:** EHS Compliance
**Applies To:** All maintenance personnel and contractors performing work on well site equipment

---

### 1. Purpose

This procedure ensures equipment is properly de-energized and isolated before maintenance work begins, protecting personnel from unexpected equipment startup or release of stored energy.

### 2. Scope

Applies to all well site equipment requiring maintenance, repair, or servicing where unexpected energization, startup, or release of stored energy could cause injury.

### 3. Required Equipment

- Approved lockout devices (padlocks, hasps, valve lockout attachments as applicable to the equipment type)
- Personal tags with the authorized employee's name and date
- Lockout/tagout log sheet

### 4. Procedure

1. **Notify affected personnel.** Inform all personnel who operate or rely on the equipment that it will be shut down for maintenance, including expected duration.
2. **Shut down the equipment** using the normal operating shutdown procedure specific to that equipment type.
3. **Isolate all energy sources.** Identify and isolate every energy source (electrical, hydraulic, pneumatic, stored mechanical) — not only the primary power source. Reference the equipment-specific energy isolation points listed in Appendix A.
4. **Apply lockout devices** to each isolation point. Each authorized employee applies their own personal lock; a shared or generic lock is not permitted.
5. **Apply tags** identifying the employee, date, and reason for lockout at each lock location.
6. **Verify isolation** by attempting to start the equipment using normal operating controls, confirming zero energy state, before beginning work. This step is not optional even when isolation seems obviously complete.
7. **Perform maintenance work.**
8. **Remove lockout devices** only after work is complete, all tools and personnel are clear of the equipment, and guards/safety devices are reinstalled. Only the employee who applied a lock may remove it; a supervisor may remove another employee's lock only following the documented lock-removal-in-absence procedure.
9. **Notify affected personnel** that the equipment is being restored to service before re-energizing.

### 5. Exceptions

Any deviation from this procedure — including group lockout for multi-person jobs — must follow the separate Group Lockout/Tagout Procedure and cannot be improvised in the field.

### 6. Training

All personnel performing lockout/tagout must complete initial training and annual refresher training, with completion logged in the training management system before independent authorization is granted.

---
*This procedure has been generalized from production SOP documentation used in an oil and gas engagement. Equipment types and specific isolation points have been altered.*
